# Decision Log

## Project Direction

Decision:
The game will be a living police career simulator, not a mission-based police game.

Reason:
The player should feel like one officer working inside a living city, not the center of the world.

---

## Core Player Role

Decision:
The player starts as nobody and builds their career through performance.

Reason:
Career progression should feel earned, not unlocked through XP alone.

---

## Game World

Decision:
The game will take place in one open world with multiple regions and departments.

Reason:
This allows the player to experience city, suburban, highway, and rural policing without needing separate maps.

---

## Departments

Decision:
Players can transfer between departments, but not instantly.

Reason:
Transfers should work like real job applications. Reputation, qualifications, openings, and history matter.

---

## Academy

Decision:
The game begins at the police academy, which serves as the tutorial.

Reason:
Players learn controls and procedures naturally through training instead of pop-up tutorials.

---

## Cutscenes

Decision:
The game should avoid traditional cutscenes whenever possible.

Reason:
The player should stay in control and experience events interactively.

---

## Time System

Decision:
The game will use accelerated in-game time, not real-time.

Reason:
Players should experience realistic day/night changes without waiting hours in real life.

---

## Work System

Decision:
The player does not start fixed missions. They clock in, work their assigned role, take breaks, clock out, and continue their career.

Reason:
This makes the game feel like a real police life/career simulator.

---

## Reports

Decision:
The game will use guided reports instead of full manual paperwork.

Reason:
Reports should affect outcomes without becoming boring or overly time-consuming.

---

## Body Cameras

Decision:
Body cameras are always recording while on duty and automatically submitted with reports.

Reason:
This keeps accountability realistic without adding unnecessary controls.

---

## Legal System

Decision:
The game will use laws based on general U.S. policing principles, not exact state-by-state laws.

Reason:
This keeps the legal system believable, consistent, and easier to understand.

---

## Failure

Decision:
There should almost never be a game over screen.

Reason:
Mistakes should create consequences, retraining, discipline, demotion, or career setbacks instead of forcing the player to reload.

---

## Qualifications

Decision:
Weapons, vehicles, and special roles are earned through training and qualifications, not money or XP unlocks.

Reason:
This makes progression feel like a real police career.

---

## Controls

Decision:
The game is primarily a PC game but must fully support controller.

Reason:
Deep gameplay should still be easy to control.

---

## Development Safety

Decision:
No full script rewrites unless approved.

Reason:
Small changes are easier to test, understand, and roll back.
