# Traffic Stops System

## Purpose

Traffic stops should be one of the core gameplay systems.

The main reason traffic stops are fun is unpredictability.

The player should never instantly know whether a stop is routine, serious, dangerous, or connected to a larger story.

Every traffic stop should begin with a question:

"What is really going on here?"

---

## Core Philosophy

Traffic stops should not feel like repeated missions.

The stop should begin before the player activates emergency lights.

A traffic stop begins when the officer notices something worth investigating.

Examples:

* Speeding
* Reckless driving
* Expired registration
* Broken equipment
* Suspicious behavior
* Vehicle matching a BOLO
* ALPR hit
* Citizen report
* Officer observation

The player chooses whether the stop is worth making.

---

## Stop Decision

Before activating emergency lights, the player should consider:

* Is the violation worth stopping?
* Is another call more important?
* Is this a safe place to initiate the stop?
* Should the plate be run first?
* Should the vehicle be followed briefly?
* Is backup needed?

The game should not force every violation to become a traffic stop.

---

## Discovery Tools

The player may discover violations or suspicious activity through:

* Visual observation
* Radar
* LIDAR
* ALPR
* Citizen reports
* BOLO alerts
* Dispatch information
* Officer experience

---

## Serious Stop Balance

Out of 100 traffic stops, around 10 to 15 should become major incidents.

Most stops should remain routine or moderately serious.

Major incidents should be uncommon enough that they feel memorable.

Examples of major outcomes:

* Wanted suspect
* Stolen vehicle
* Kidnapping victim
* Illegal weapon
* Large drug transport
* Pursuit
* Connection to a larger investigation

---

## Driver Behavior

Driver behavior should depend on the person, situation, vehicle, and environment.

Drivers may:

* Pull over immediately
* Look for a safer place to stop
* Delay briefly
* Act nervous
* Refuse to stop
* Flee

Behavior should not be purely random. It should be influenced by the citizen’s current goal, personality, criminal history, stress level, and what is happening in their life.

---

## Passenger Behavior

Passengers should matter.

Passengers may:

* Stay quiet
* Be nervous
* Interrupt the driver
* Record the officer
* Hide something
* Warn the driver not to talk
* Try to flee
* Give helpful information
* Escalate the situation

Passengers should not only exist to create danger. Most passengers should be normal people.

---

## Hidden Story Layer

Every traffic stop should have three layers:

1. The reason for the stop
2. What the officer discovers during the stop
3. The hidden story behind the situation

Examples:

A driver may be speeding because:

* They are late for work
* They are scared
* They are impaired
* They are fleeing a crime
* They are rushing someone to the hospital
* They simply made a mistake

The player uncovers the truth through investigation and decisions.

---

## Legal Justification

The game should track what the officer is legally justified in doing.

The player should not see a simple suspicion number.

Instead, the interaction system should show what actions are currently justified.

Example:

If the only issue is speeding, the player may be able to warn or cite the driver, but a vehicle search is not justified.

If the officer observes facts that create probable cause, the vehicle search option becomes legally justified.

The game may still allow unjustified actions, but those actions can create consequences later.

---

## Consent Searches

The player can request consent to search a vehicle.

If consent is denied, the player needs legal justification to continue.

Searching without consent or legal justification may result in:

* Evidence being excluded
* Case dismissal
* Supervisor review
* Discipline
* Loss of trust
* Training requirement

---

## Case Outcomes

Arresting someone is not the end of the case.

The legal process may review:

* Reason for stop
* Search justification
* Arrest justification
* Report accuracy
* Body camera evidence
* Chain of custody

If the officer acted incorrectly, evidence may be thrown out and the case may fail.

The game should explain why this happened so the player learns from the outcome.

---

## Player Memory Support

The player should not have to remember every past interaction.

The MDT should help.

When running a person, plate, or vehicle, the MDT may show:

* Previous stops
* Prior arrests
* Past warnings
* Known officer safety concerns
* Previous interaction with the player
* Related case history

This makes the city feel persistent without forcing the player to memorize everything.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Pursuits

Pursuits should be rare enough to feel exciting, but common enough that players know they can happen.

Around 10 out of 100 traffic stops may involve some type of fleeing behavior, but not all fleeing should become a full vehicle pursuit.

Fleeing behavior may include:

* Delaying before stopping
* Refusing to stop
* Stopping and then fleeing
* Driver running on foot
* Passenger running
* Driver abandoning the vehicle
* Full vehicle pursuit

Pursuits should be about risk management, not simply chasing until the player wins.

The player should consider:

* Reason for the pursuit
* Traffic conditions
* Weather
* Time of day
* Pedestrians
* School zones
* Speed
* Public safety
* Department policy

When a vehicle flees, dispatch is automatically notified so the player does not have to fight menus while driving.

The player may still update dispatch with:

* Direction of travel
* Speed
* Traffic conditions
* Suspect vehicle description
* Reason for pursuit
* Backup needs

Suspects can escape.

If a suspect escapes, the game should remember the vehicle, person, and incident. Later, the MDT or ALPR may alert the player that the same vehicle or person fled from a previous stop.

Pursuits may end when:

* Suspect stops voluntarily
* Suspect crashes
* Officer performs PIT maneuver
* Spike strips are used
* Suspect bails on foot
* Suspect escapes
* Supervisor terminates pursuit
* Vehicle becomes disabled
* Another unit catches the suspect later

Backup units should help during pursuits.

Backup may:

* Join the pursuit
* Take secondary position
* Block traffic
* Set a perimeter
* Search the area
* Handle passengers
* Transport suspects
* Deploy spike strips if qualified

A supervisor can approve, continue, or terminate a pursuit.

If a supervisor orders the player to terminate a pursuit, the player can continue anyway, but doing so creates serious consequences unless there is a strong justification.

PIT maneuvers, spike strips, and advanced pursuit tactics require training, qualification, and sometimes supervisor authorization.

Patrol cars, suspect vehicles, and civilian vehicles can take damage during pursuits.

Civilian traffic should react to lights, sirens, and pursuits. Drivers may pull over, panic brake, fail to yield, block the road, or cause accidents.

Pursuit consequences should depend on what actually happened.

If the pursuit was reasonable and nothing bad happened, the player should not be punished.

If the pursuit caused injury, property damage, policy violations, or ignored supervisor orders, the department may review the incident.

## Traffic Stop Flow

A traffic stop should follow a clear but flexible flow.

Basic flow:

1. Officer notices a vehicle or violation.
2. Officer decides whether the stop is worth making.
3. Officer targets the vehicle.
4. Officer activates emergency lights.
5. Driver reacts and pulls over, delays, stops unsafely, or flees.
6. Officer runs the plate.
7. Officer decides how to approach.
8. Officer contacts the driver.
9. Officer investigates.
10. Officer decides the outcome.
11. Report is generated.
12. The world remembers the interaction.

---

## Vehicle Targeting

The game should use both automatic and manual targeting.

The game should normally detect the vehicle in front of the patrol car.

The player should also be able to manually target a vehicle if traffic is crowded or the automatic system targets the wrong vehicle.

---

## Pulling Over

Driver behavior depends on the driver, situation, location, traffic, and risk.

Drivers may:

* Pull over safely
* Look for a better stopping location
* Stop in an unsafe location
* Delay briefly
* Refuse to stop
* Flee

---

## Unsafe Stop Locations

If the driver stops somewhere unsafe, the player can use the PA system to order them to move forward to a safer location.

Examples of unsafe locations:

* Bridge
* Sharp curve
* Busy highway shoulder
* Intersection
* Railroad crossing
* Blind hill

This gives the player officer-safety decisions without adding confusing controls.

---

## Before Approaching

The normal recommended process is:

1. Stop the vehicle.
2. Run the plate.
3. Review basic vehicle information.
4. Decide how to approach.

The player can choose differently if needed, but the game should teach that running the plate before approach is usually smart.

---

## Approach Options

The player should choose how to approach the vehicle.

Options may include:

* Driver-side approach
* Passenger-side approach
* Wait in patrol car
* Request backup
* Use spotlight
* Use PA system
* High-risk approach

Different approaches should matter depending on the situation.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
## Driver Contact and Interaction

When the officer reaches the driver window, the officer should automatically introduce themselves.

Example:

“Hello, I’m Officer [Last Name] with [Department Name].”

The player should not need to press a separate greeting button.

After the introduction, the interaction wheel should show useful options such as:

* Ask for documents
* Explain reason for stop
* Ask questions
* Observe driver
* Observe vehicle
* Use handheld device
* Request backup
* End contact

Dialogue should use a mixed system.

Some common lines can be exact dialogue options, while tone and intent affect how the officer speaks.

Examples of tone:

* Professional
* Firm
* Friendly
* Suspicious
* Calm/de-escalating

Drivers should react based on personality, mood, criminal history, stress, current situation, and past police contact.

---

## Documents

The player can ask for:

* Driver’s license
* Registration
* Insurance

The driver physically hands over documents, but the system should remain simple.

After documents are received, the information appears on the officer’s handheld device.

Drivers may fail to provide documents.

Examples:

* No license
* Suspended license
* Fake ID
* Borrowed vehicle
* Stolen vehicle
* Forgot wallet
* Refuses to provide ID
* Expired insurance
* Expired registration

---

## Handheld Device

The player should not have to return to the patrol car many times during one stop.

The officer has a department-issued handheld device for quick checks.

The handheld device can be used to:

* Run a person
* Run a driver’s license
* Check warrants
* Check registration
* Check insurance
* View previous contacts
* Request backup
* Check call notes
* Add quick notes

The patrol car MDT is still used for deeper information, reports, maps, unit availability, and full call details.

---

## Stop Outcomes

Traffic stop outcomes should remain simple.

The main outcomes are:

* No action
* Warning
* Citation
* Arrest

The game can handle smaller details in the background, but the player-facing choices should stay clean.

---

## Ordering Occupants Out

The player can order the driver out of the vehicle when justified.

Examples:

* DUI suspicion
* Arrest
* Safety concern
* Suspicious behavior
* Vehicle search
* Need to separate occupants

The player can also order passengers out when justified.

Example:

A passenger keeps reaching under the seat.

Ordering people out without a good reason may create complaints, supervisor review, or career consequences.

---

## Vehicle Searches

Vehicle searches should be simple.

The player chooses “Search Vehicle.”

The officer performs the search.

The game returns the result.

Example search result:

Found:

* Open alcohol container
* Unregistered handgun under passenger seat
* Cash in center console
* Backpack belonging to rear passenger

The search action should be simple, but the outcome can still create decisions.

Some evidence is obvious.

Examples:

* Firearm
* Open alcohol
* Stolen plate
* Bloody clothing

Some evidence may require testing or processing.

Examples:

* Pills
* White powder
* Unknown substance
* Fingerprints
* DNA
* Fake documents

---

## DUI and Impairment

DUI suspicion can come from clues.

Examples:

* Swerving
* Speeding
* Running red lights
* Slurred speech
* Alcohol smell
* Open container
* Confusion
* Poor driving
* Driver behavior

The player can use a breath test.

Example:

Breath Test Result: 0.092%

Game Legal Limit: 0.080%

The breath test is helpful evidence, but it is not always the only evidence.

Medical issues can sometimes mimic impairment.

Examples:

* Diabetic emergency
* Head injury
* Medication reaction
* Exhaustion
* Panic attack
* Stroke-like symptoms

If a driver is speeding because of a medical emergency, the stop can turn into a medical response instead of enforcement.

---

## Arrests During Traffic Stops

Arrests should be simple but interactive.

A normal arrest flow should be:

1. Order person out
2. Turn around
3. Handcuff
4. Search person
5. Place in patrol car

Suspects may resist arrest depending on personality, criminal history, warrants, fear, substance use, current crime, backup presence, and weapon involvement.

Resistance may include:

* Verbal argument
* Pulling away
* Running
* Fighting
* Complying at first, then fleeing

After arrest, the player may transport the suspect or request a transport unit.

The player decides what happens to the suspect vehicle.

Options may include:

* Release vehicle to valid licensed passenger
* Tow vehicle
* Search vehicle if legally justified
* Inventory vehicle before tow
* Leave vehicle parked legally
* Release to owner later

Arrest reports should be mostly auto-generated by the game.

The player should confirm key information, add optional notes, and submit.

Reports should be accountability checkpoints, not busywork.
