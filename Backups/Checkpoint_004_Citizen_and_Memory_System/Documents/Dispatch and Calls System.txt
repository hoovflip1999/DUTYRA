# Dispatch and Calls System

## Purpose

The dispatch system creates, assigns, updates, and tracks calls for service.

Calls should not feel like random missions.

A call should feel like something happened in the city, someone reported it, dispatch received information, and officers responded.

The player is not the center of the world. Other officers can handle calls if the player is busy, unavailable, or chooses not to respond.

---

## Core Call Flow

Every call should follow this structure:

1. Something happens in the city.
2. A citizen, officer, business, or system reports it.
3. Dispatch receives information.
4. The information may be accurate, incomplete, or wrong.
5. Units are assigned or the call becomes available.
6. The situation can change over time.
7. The player or another unit responds.
8. The call is investigated.
9. The officer makes decisions.
10. The call is resolved, escalates, or continues later.
11. The world remembers the outcome.

---

## Call Creation

Call creation uses a hybrid system.

Most calls should come from the city simulation.

Example:

Citizen drinks at bar.

Citizen drives home.

Citizen crashes into a parked vehicle.

Neighbor calls 911.

Dispatch creates a crash or DUI call.

Some larger events can be designed.

Examples:

* City festival
* Storm
* Parade
* Election event
* Sports event
* Large emergency

Designed events should still feel like part of the world instead of scripted missions.

---

## Dispatch Information Accuracy

Dispatch information can be accurate, incomplete, or wrong.

The call type represents what was reported, not always what is actually happening.

Example:

Dispatch reports a suspicious person, but it may actually be:

* Nothing
* A burglary attempt
* A medical emergency
* A lost person
* A wanted person
* A false report

This keeps calls unpredictable.

---

## Call Assignment

The game uses both assigned calls and available calls.

Priority calls may be directly assigned by dispatch.

Lower-priority calls may appear on the MDT as available calls.

The player can accept some calls, decline some calls, or recommend another unit when appropriate.

---

## Other Units

Other officers can handle calls.

If the player ignores a call, is busy, or is outside the area, another unit may respond.

The city continues operating even when the player is not involved.

---

## Call Updates

Calls can change while the player is responding.

Example:

Original call:

Noise complaint.

Update:

Caller now reports people fighting outside.

Update:

One person left in a red pickup truck.

Call updates make responding feel alive instead of simply driving to a marker.

---

## Priority Levels

The game uses three call priority levels.

### Priority 1 — Emergency

Examples:

* Officer needs assistance
* Active shooter
* Armed robbery
* Major crash with injuries
* Shots fired

### Priority 2 — Urgent

Examples:

* Domestic disturbance
* Burglary in progress
* Assault
* DUI driver
* Stolen vehicle sighting

### Priority 3 — Routine

Examples:

* Noise complaint
* Parking complaint
* Minor crash
* Welfare check
* Suspicious person

---

## Lights and Sirens

The player chooses whether to use lights and sirens.

If the player does not use lights and sirens:

* Traffic does not move over
* Response is slower
* The player may arrive late
* The situation may change before arrival

If the player uses lights and sirens:

* Traffic tries to pull over
* Response is faster
* Crash risk increases
* Bad driving can be reviewed later

Using lights and sirens should be a decision, not an automatic button press.

---

## Declining Calls

Declining calls depends on priority and unit availability.

Priority 1 calls usually cannot be declined unless the player is already handling another serious incident.

Priority 2 calls require a good reason to decline.

Priority 3 calls can usually be declined if another unit is available.

If no other unit is available and the player refuses an appropriate assignment, the player may face supervisor review or discipline.

---

## Call Holding

Calls can wait if no unit is available.

While holding, a call may:

* Resolve itself
* Get worse
* Generate another call
* Lower public trust
* Be handled by another unit later

Example:

Priority 3 noise complaint.

Holding for 14 minutes.

No available units.

---

## Unit Availability Display

The MDT should show unit availability.

Example:

Available Units:

Unit 12 — Available

Unit 18 — On Call

Unit 21 — Transporting

Unit 30 — Available

Pending Calls:

P1 — Robbery in Progress

P2 — Domestic Disturbance

P3 — Noise Complaint

This helps the player understand why dispatch is assigning certain calls.

---

# Version 1 Call Types

Version 1 should focus on a small number of call types with multiple possible outcomes.

The Version 1 call types are:

1. Traffic enforcement / traffic stops
2. Suspicious person
3. Noise complaint
4. Welfare check
5. Minor crash
6. Shoplifting
7. Domestic disturbance
8. DUI driver

The goal is not to create 100 call types.

The goal is to create 8 call types that can play out many different ways.

---

## Traffic Enforcement / Traffic Stops

Purpose:

Make everyday patrol unpredictable.

Traffic enforcement is usually officer-initiated.

The player may notice:

* Speeding
* Reckless driving
* Expired registration
* Broken lights
* ALPR hit
* BOLO match
* Suspicious driving

Possible outcomes:

* No action
* Warning
* Citation
* Arrest
* DUI
* Stolen vehicle
* Wanted person
* Medical emergency
* Pursuit
* Evidence tied to another case

Design rule:

Traffic stops should feel routine most of the time, but always carry the possibility of becoming something bigger.

---

## Suspicious Person

Purpose:

A flexible call that can be harmless, strange, serious, or dangerous.

Dispatch gives:

* Location
* Caller statement
* General description
* Reported behavior
* Basic overview

Possible outcomes:

* Nothing wrong
* Lost person
* Waiting for ride
* Mental health issue
* Trespassing
* Vehicle burglary attempt
* Stalking
* Wanted person
* Person casing homes or businesses
* Armed suspect

Design rule:

The person may not still be there when the officer arrives. Witnesses, cameras, or later MDT alerts can continue the investigation.

---

## Noise Complaint

Purpose:

A low-priority call that can stay simple or unexpectedly escalate.

Dispatch gives:

* Location
* Caller statement
* Type of noise
* Time reported
* Whether caller wants contact
* Prior calls at location

Possible outcomes:

* Loud TV
* Party
* Kids playing
* Neighbor dispute
* False complaint
* Fight
* Domestic disturbance
* Underage drinking
* Drugs
* Medical emergency
* Gunshots mistaken as fireworks

Design rule:

Repeat locations matter. A house with repeated noise calls should eventually become known to officers and flagged in the MDT.

---

## Welfare Check

Purpose:

A call type that can be calm, emotional, medical, dangerous, or tragic.

Dispatch gives:

* Location
* Who requested the check
* Reason for concern
* Last known contact
* Person description if known
* Prior calls at location

Possible harmless outcomes:

* Person was sleeping
* Phone died
* Person forgot to call family
* Person is out of town
* Miscommunication
* Neighbor overreacted

Possible serious outcomes:

* Medical emergency
* Mental health crisis
* Domestic violence
* Death investigation
* Child neglect
* Person trapped or injured
* Armed person in crisis

Player options:

* Knock
* Call out
* Contact caller
* Look through windows if justified
* Request EMS
* Request fire department
* Request supervisor
* Force entry if legally justified
* Clear call if person is okay

Forced entry rule:

The player cannot force entry just because someone does not answer.

Forced entry needs justification, such as screams, visible injury, smell of smoke, medical distress, child crying alone, or strong evidence of danger.

Design rule:

Welfare checks should teach patience and judgment. The right answer is not always arrest. Sometimes the right answer is EMS, backup, or simply confirming someone is safe.

---

## Minor Crash

Purpose:

A call type that teaches investigation, traffic control, report decisions, and hidden causes.

Dispatch gives:

* Location
* Number of vehicles if known
* Injuries reported or unknown
* Whether traffic is blocked
* Caller statement
* Weather or road condition if relevant

Possible outcomes:

* Simple property damage crash
* One driver has no insurance
* One driver has suspended license
* DUI discovered
* Hit-and-run
* Road rage
* Medical issue caused crash
* Vehicle was stolen
* Wanted person involved
* Minor crash becomes fight

Player options:

* Check for injuries
* Request EMS or fire
* Control traffic
* Interview drivers
* Interview witnesses
* Run licenses and plates
* Determine crash cause
* Issue citation if justified
* Arrest if crime exists
* Request tow
* Complete crash report

Design rule:

Minor crashes should not always be boring paperwork. They should sometimes reveal bigger problems, but most should stay normal.

---

## Shoplifting

Purpose:

A beginner-friendly call involving witnesses, suspects, evidence, business owners, and discretion.

Dispatch gives:

* Store location
* Caller statement
* Suspect description
* Whether suspect is detained or left
* What was stolen if known
* Whether security is involved

Possible outcomes:

* Suspect still on scene
* Suspect left before arrival
* False accusation
* Employee misunderstanding
* Juvenile shoplifter
* Repeat offender
* Wanted person
* Suspect flees
* Suspect has drugs or weapons
* Organized retail theft connection

Player options:

* Talk to employee
* Talk to suspect
* Review store statement
* Check security footage summary
* Run suspect
* Recover stolen property
* Warning
* Citation
* Arrest
* Trespass warning
* Report only

Design rule:

Not every shoplifting call should become an arrest. The player should consider value stolen, suspect age, criminal history, cooperation, store preference, and evidence.

---

## Domestic Disturbance

Purpose:

One of the most serious Version 1 calls. It should be unpredictable and require good judgment.

Dispatch gives:

* Location
* Caller statement
* Relationship if known
* Reported violence or argument
* Weapons mentioned or unknown
* Children present if known
* Prior calls at address

Possible outcomes:

* Verbal argument only
* One person leaves voluntarily
* Mutual argument
* Domestic assault
* Injured victim
* Children present
* Protective order violation
* Suspect fled before arrival
* Weapon involved
* False or exaggerated report
* Victim afraid to speak

Player options:

* Request backup
* Knock and announce
* Separate people
* Interview each person
* Check for injuries
* Check children or other occupants
* Run names
* Check prior calls
* Arrest if probable cause exists
* Request EMS
* Request supervisor
* Complete report

Design rule:

Domestic calls should not be treated like simple arguments. They should require caution, separation, observation, and good documentation.

---

## DUI Driver

Purpose:

A traffic-related call that tests observation, driving behavior, breath testing, and medical awareness.

Dispatch gives:

* Last known location
* Vehicle description
* Driving behavior reported
* Direction of travel
* Caller statement
* Plate if known

Possible outcomes:

* Driver is sober
* Driver is tired
* Driver is distracted
* Driver is impaired by alcohol
* Driver is impaired by drugs
* Medical emergency
* Driver refuses test
* Open container found
* Crash before arrival
* Vehicle gone before arrival

Player options:

* Locate vehicle
* Observe driving
* Initiate traffic stop
* Speak with driver
* Observe impairment clues
* Order driver out if justified
* Breath test
* Request EMS if medical issue suspected
* Warning or citation if minor issue
* Arrest if justified
* Tow or release vehicle decision

Design rule:

DUI should not just be “breath test equals arrest.” The player should observe behavior, consider medical issues, use the breath test, and make a decision based on the full situation.

---

## Call Frequency Balance

Version 1 call frequency should roughly follow this structure:

Common:

* Traffic stops
* Suspicious person
* Noise complaint
* Minor crash

Moderate:

* Welfare check
* Shoplifting
* DUI driver

Less common but serious:

* Domestic disturbance

Most days should not feel like nonstop chaos, but every shift should have the chance to surprise the player.
